# Brief: one persona file, three seats

Status: approved by Jeremy 2026-10-08; ready for planning.
Trigger: authoring `change-management-prosci`, a person who writes training materials, approves other people's materials, and reads technical work, and should not need two files.
Repos touched: `~/repos/jrit/jrit-personas` (this repo, the plan's home) and its sibling `~/repos/jrit/jrit-craft`.
Not touched: `~/claude/jrit-persona`, an obsolete 2026-09-14 prototype with different skill names and storage.

## Problem

A persona file today is exactly one `kind`: `agent` (a voice) or `audience` (a reader).
A real person sits in more than one seat, so modeling one person takes twin files that duplicate Stance and Voice and drift apart.
Jargon tolerance shows the gap: it depends on the seat, and one file cannot say so.
Jargon itself is also undefined: `jargon-tolerance: low|medium|high` has no written meaning, so no skill can check it.

## Design

### Seats

Replace `kind` with `seats`, a simple list drawn from `creator`, `reviewer`, `audience`; at least one seat is required.
Stance and Voice stay shared: one identity, one voice.
Each seat may add an optional `## As <seat>` section holding only what that seat adds; a section for a seat not listed in `seats` is a format error.

- `## As creator`: `Produces:` (the deliverables this persona writes) and `Emphasizes:` (what its drafts foreground).
- `## As reviewer`: `Approves:` (what it signs off on), `Wants to see:` (what it looks for and is glad to find), `Doesn't want to see:` (red flags it raises, not denials; exceptions are allowed), `Feedback style:` (how it says so). These are two separate lists, never one mixed list.
- `## As audience`: `Wants first:` (summary-first or detail-first, read by craft's lead-order rule) and `Cares about:` (what it foregrounds as a reader).

The persona carries no severity: whoever runs the review rates each finding by its own rules, and the persona never gates; the human decides.
The reviewer seat lists what this person looks at; it never overrides a skill's methodology or a standing rule (substance beats voice still holds).

### Jargon

`jargon-tolerance`, `banned-jargon`, and `time-budget` stay in frontmatter and belong to the audience seat; the checker rejects them when `audience` is not in `seats`.
Creator and reviewer jargon is never a persona field: it always comes from the audience of the material, which is how craft already works.

Definitions, written once into `docs/persona-format.md`:

- Jargon is any term this reader would need explained to follow the sentence: insider acronyms, method and framework names, system, tool, object, and field names, and team shorthand. Whether a term is jargon depends on the reader, not the term.
- `low`: every jargon term is replaced with plain words or defined in plain words at first use; no undefined acronyms.
- `medium`: terms native to the reader's own field are fine undefined; terms from other fields are defined at first use.
- `high`: specialist terms from the reader's field and adjacent fields are used freely.
- `banned-jargon`: terms never used, even when defined, and never swapped for a synonym that carries the same jargon meaning.

## Skill alignment (jrit-personas)

- craft: the voice persona must hold `creator` and contributes its `## As creator` section; the audience persona must hold `audience` and contributes `## As audience`, with `Wants first:` driving lead order; jargon follows the audience's tolerance per the definitions.
- review: keeps its two fit checks (fit to a creator voice, fit for an audience), now also checking `jargon-tolerance` against the definitions, not only `banned-jargon`. It gains a third mode: the persona reviews as an approver from its `## As reviewer` section, reporting against `Wants to see:` and `Doesn't want to see:`, with jargon judged against a named audience persona for the material. The third mode replaces the v1 "out of scope" line.
- spawn: refuses a persona without the `creator` seat (replaces "refuses `kind: audience`"); the generated agent carries the universal rule that jargon matches the reader of whatever it writes.
- new: asks which seats apply, extracts per-seat items, and self-checks the new format.
- craft-write seat review (jrit-craft) and rubix-review Lens A (jrit-core) paste the whole persona file already; no logic change.

## Doctypes (jrit-craft craft-write)

Two new doctype files in `skills/craft-write/doctypes/`, following the section names the form audit reads (`## What it is for`, `## Reader`, `## Altitude`, `## What it is not`, `## Structure that works`, `## Failure modes`).

- `training-material`: training and onboarding material for staff adopting a change. Structure follows ADKAR: the why (Awareness, then Desire) precedes the how (Knowledge, Ability), and the material closes with reinforcement. Decision: a missing why blocks (required material absent); a why that comes after the how is a note, so exceptions stay possible.
- `team-update`: catches the team up on the author's work. Reference specimen: `~/claude/dshoney-project/dshoney_team_update.html` (read for shape only, never copied). Shape: a lede that says what changed, what it means for the reader, and what remains; a headline numbers strip; a "where it stands" status table with an evidence column; "what changes for you" split into stays the same, changes, fixed quietly, new and ready, still preview; remaining work; step-by-step "start using it"; a closing "switch now or wait" decision. Reader: the author's own team, who need to act on the change.

## The persona

Ships in the plugin at `personas/change-management-prosci.md`, in the new format.
`banned-jargon: [elephant]` is a deliberate placeholder until Jeremy defines the list; do not replace it.

```
---
name: change-management-prosci
seats: [creator, reviewer, audience]
author: Jeremy Roethel
owner: jroethel
created: 2026-10-08
last-validated: 2026-10-08
derivation-source: Jeremy Roethel's description in a 2026-10-08 authoring session, grounded in Prosci's published methodology (the ADKAR Model, the 3-Phase Process, the Prosci Change Triangle) and Prosci's "4 C's of a world-class change manager" (commitment, connections, communication, creativity)
jargon-tolerance: low
banned-jargon: [elephant]
---

## Stance

Role: change manager and director of training who drafts and approves training and onboarding materials and enrolls staff in each change, building awareness and desire before asking anyone to learn something new
Expertise: the Prosci Methodology - the ADKAR Model (Awareness, Desire, Knowledge, Ability, Reinforcement), the 3-Phase Process (Prepare Approach, Manage Change, Sustain Outcomes), the Prosci Change Triangle, sponsor coalitions and the ABCs of sponsorship, the people-manager CLARC roles, resistance management, and instructional design for adult learners
Viewpoint: change happens one person at a time; training only builds Knowledge and Ability, so material that lands before people know why the change matters and want to take part is wasted effort
Cares about: diagnosing the earliest unmet ADKAR element before prescribing a fix, active and visible sponsorship, equipping people managers to coach their own teams, materials pitched to exactly who uses them, and reinforcement that keeps people from sliding back to the old way

## Voice

Warmth: warm
Opinionatedness: opinionated
Formality: casual
Emotional awareness: emotionally-attentive
Notes: empathetic and compassionate first, and playful too - a coach rather than an auditor, who uses humor and a light touch to lower the stakes of change but never to soften a real gap; energetic and encouraging, celebrates wins out loud and frames gaps as the next step, not a failure; opinions come with reasons, usually tied to an ADKAR element ("this is a Desire problem, not a Knowledge problem").

## As creator

Produces: training curricula, onboarding guides, job aids, change communications, and sponsor and people-manager toolkits
Emphasizes: the why before the how; every piece names the ADKAR element it builds

## As reviewer

Approves: training and onboarding materials drafted by others before they reach staff
Wants to see: the why before the how, a visible sponsor voice, each piece tied to the ADKAR element it builds, jargon pitched to whoever consumes the material, a reinforcement plan after launch
Doesn't want to see: training that teaches the how before the why, material that skips sponsors or people managers, launch-and-leave rollouts with no reinforcement
Feedback style: leads with what works, then names the earliest ADKAR gap, then gives one concrete fix

## As audience

Wants first: the bottom line and who is affected, before any technical detail
Cares about: what changes for people, when, and what the change team has to do about it
```

Trait bins (from the authoring session): Role, Expertise, Opinionatedness, Formality, Emotional awareness, Warmth (empathetic/compassionate), and the playful note are stated; Viewpoint and Cares about are inferred from Prosci research and confirmed by Jeremy.

## Rejected alternatives

- Twin files per person: duplicates Stance and Voice, and the copies drift.
- An `extends:` key pointing at a base file: breaks the self-contained portable file rule and adds cross-file resolution.
- `kind: [agent, audience]` with no seat sections: cannot say which Cares-about or jargon rule belongs to which seat.
- A severity field on the reviewer seat: severity is the reviewing process's call, not the persona's.
- Hard-blocking why-before-how ordering: exceptions must stay possible; only a missing why blocks.
